# YouTube affiliate candidates — Go Top SEO

Research date: **2026-11-12**. All figures were read off YouTube itself (search results, channel About tabs, channel Videos tabs and individual watch-page descriptions) — not from third-party "best SEO tools" blog posts, which are mostly AI-generated affiliate spam.

Offer being pitched: 35% recurring for the customer's life, 40% from ten concurrent paying referrals, plans $79–$649/mo. Programme page: https://www.gotopseo.com/affiliates

**Nobody on this list has been contacted.** No sign-in, no subscribe, no comment, no like was performed. Where YouTube hides the business email behind its "Sign in to see email address" button, that is stated plainly — no email has been guessed or inferred.

## Best five, and why

1. **Top SaaS & AI Tools** (130K subs) — the single closest match. They have already published a full tutorial/demo of *Soro*, an "AI SEO tool that writes and publishes blog posts on autopilot", i.e. Go Top SEO's exact category, complete with a chapter titled "Soro Pricing and Affiliate Program" and a standing affiliate disclaimer. They publish a new tool demo most days, views run 2–12K per video, and the About tab lists a **public** business email. They monetise by being paid to demo SaaS; Go Top SEO is a drop-in next episode.
2. **Tim The SEO Guru** (17K subs) — already carries an affiliate link *and a personal coupon code* for Arvow, the closest competing AI SEO writing tool ("Use code Tim10 for 10% off"). Near-daily uploads, local-business framing, and a sister channel (Tim's AEO Tips, 6.3K) running the same Arvow promotion with a second code. He has demonstrably said yes to this exact offer shape before, so the pitch is "same deal, better terms, plus Maps and AI-answer tracking".
3. **The Savvy Couple — Kelan & Brittany** (40.6K subs) — professional recurring-SaaS affiliates: FirstPromoter links for *Merchynt* (AI Google Maps / local SEO), GoHighLevel recurring links, an explicit affiliate disclosure, bonus stacks, and they run an "Affiliate Cashflow Academy". Their audience is agency owners who buy recurring software to resell — the exact buyer for $79–$649/mo plans at 40%.
4. **Caleb Ulku** (50.6K subs) — builds AI local-SEO agency content for agency owners, posts every few days, and already promotes recurring SaaS with negotiated terms (LeadSnap at 50% off for 3 months via his own signup link). His videos are long walkthroughs of tools that build and rank local sites, which is how Go Top SEO demos best.
5. **Business Solution** (25.7K subs) — a pure SEO-software comparison channel. Every tool mentioned goes through a cloaked affiliate redirect on their own domain (`businessolution.org/get/...`) with negotiated free trials and discounts, and the format is "7 Best AI SEO Tools — Comparison & Full Demo". Publishes several reviews a day.

Just outside the five, and worth approaching in the same wave: **Portable Entrepreneur** (sells recurring tools to SEO/web-design resellers, 66%-off affiliate deal on GMB Everywhere), **🔥 TOP Digital Products** (33K subs, 4–8K views a review, affiliate disclaimer on every video) and **My First Website** (a whole affiliate stack incl. Semrush and a Localo review).

## Explicitly excluded, and why

These came up repeatedly in the same searches and should **not** be pitched:

- **Tool vendors running their own channels:** Ahrefs, SE Ranking, Rank Math, Whitespark, Localo, Merchynt, Keyword.com Rank Tracker, Surfer Academy, Exploding Topics, Squarespace Help, CrowdReply (`crowdreply.io?via=…` is their own product), Vicin, GBPPromote, LocalRank, Soro.
- **Creators who ship a competing AI SEO product**, so their "review" slot is already sold to themselves: **Alex Moreau / MediaRyze** (Ryze AI), **Vasco's SEO Tips** (Arvow), **Alex's SEO Tips** (SEOForge), **Galaxy Growth Media** (RocketRank).
- **Terry Bay / Tutorials With Terry** (10.7K) — does carry Semrush affiliate links and ranked "Best SEO Tools for Website 2026", but the channel has pivoted to Amazon gift listicles ("14 Best Amazon Gifts for Men", posted daily). Wrong audience now.

## The review-spam cluster (what to ignore)

The "SEO software review" search is where AI-generated affiliate spam concentrates. Four near-identical "Soro SEO Review 2026" videos came from channels with no audience and no history: **SEO with Charlie** (7 subscribers, created Oct 2025), **Technically Matt** (32 subscribers), **Digital Tech Reviews 24** (6 subscribers, created Sep 2026), **TopPickTesters** (4 views on the video that ranked), plus **Daniel \| Tech & Data** and **Daniel Davidson**. They rank because the phrase is uncontested, not because anyone watches. Paying them would buy indexed pages, not referrals.

## Which searches actually worked

- **Produced real creators:** `AI SEO tools` (best single query — surfaced Tim The SEO Guru, Nico, Caleb Ulku, Jesse Cunningham, Conor Martin, TOP Digital Products, Top SaaS & AI Tools), `Google Business Profile ranking` (Ranking Academy, Zanet Design, Portable Entrepreneur, Sterling Sky, Steve Hunsaker), `local SEO tools review` (Sterling Sky, My First Website, The Savvy Couple, Daniel Agrici), `best SEO tools 2026` (Nathan Gotch, Kasra Dash, Marketer Milk, George Vlasyev, Business Solution).
- **Mostly spam or dead:** `SEO software review` — the top ten is the Soro-review spam cluster above, with only Marketer Milk, Nathan Gotch and Tooltester salvageable. `rank tracker review` — dominated by 5–13-year-old AppSumo lifetime-deal reviews; almost nothing current.
- **Wrong audience:** `programmatic SEO` and `SEO for agencies` returned developers, Hormozi-style agency-scaling content and course sellers rather than software reviewers. Matt Diggity and Edward Sturm were the only useful finds there.

## Honest confidence

Of the 26 rows, **12 are solid**: #1–12 all have an affiliate or sponsor link verified in a real video description, a tool-review format, and an upload within the last week. #13–20 are real, active, well-matched channels where I could not find existing affiliate links — they are sponsorship or partnership conversations, not drop-in affiliate signups. #21–26 are included with their weakness stated (lifetime-deal audience, stale uploads, or an unread Videos tab).

Business emails: one public email in an About tab (Top SaaS & AI Tools), one public in a channel description (Jesse Cunningham). For every other channel marked "hidden", YouTube shows a "Sign in to see email address" button that was not clicked. Four channels (Tim's AEO Tips, Michael Tandarich, Edward Sturm, Technically Matt) show no business-email field at all. Reaching the hidden ones means using the website/contact form in the Links column, or signing in — none of these addresses should be guessed.
